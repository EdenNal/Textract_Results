We are writing to submit our manuscript, \textbf{``Evaluation of automated
table extraction for historical infectious-disease surveillance data,''} for consideration as a
Research Article in \textit{PLOS ONE}.

Most historical public-health data remain locked in scanned paper documents, limiting large-scale epidemiological research. We evaluate Amazon Textract, a cloud-based machine-learning document-extraction service, as an alternative to manual data entry, applying it to the Canadian Disease Incidence Dataset (CANDID), an archive of weekly disease counts for Canadian provinces and territories from 1956 to 1958. After a documented post-processing pipeline, automated extraction achieved 92.9\% content-only cell-level agreement with the manually entered reference among cells numeric in that reference (95.6\% under a stricter denominator); the epidemiologically relevant statistics we derive from the extracted data (cross-correlations, epidemic peak timing, and growth rates) were generally similar to those from the manual data, with quantified, documented differences. This work sits within \textit{PLOS ONE}'s broad scope as a transparent, reproducible evaluation of a digital tool with direct implications for public health research practice, and we believe it will interest readers in digital health informatics, public health surveillance, and mathematical epidemiology.
